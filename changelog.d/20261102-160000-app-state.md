**Towards several Apps in one process: every global has its class, and an App's state moves into `App`** (docs/architecture.md, "Where state lives"). `tools/globals_allowed.txt` now classes the 559 globals of `src/browser`, `src/page`, `src/style`, `src/layout`, `src/media` and `src/js` as `const`, `process`, `thread`, `app`, `window`, `page` or `test`, and the lint (`test_all`) holds `thread` to `@(thread_local)` both ways. 59 of the shell's globals became fields of `App` (the history, the icons, the back/forward cache, the downloads, the search engines, the palette, Setup, the commands, the preferences' watches...), reached through the `^App` the code is given or a callback's data (a job's, a timer's, a window subclass's); `the_app` is the App of its thread and is read on 109 lines instead of 156. The live pages, the navigables and the frames waiting to close are their thread's (an App runs its pages on the thread of its loop) and page and navigable ids are unique in the process: two Apps' pages on two threads each see their own (`two_apps_pages_on_two_threads_keep_to_their_own`). Fixed on the way: a finished desktop-integration job read its struct after freeing it. Page loads and frames measured before and after on six pages of `examples/site` (median of ten interleaved runs each, wall clock and CPU cycles): the same within the noise.
