**Middle-click autoscroll** (`browser.scroll.autoscroll.*`): a press of the wheel on the page drops an anchor and the pointer's distance from it scrolls the region under it, the farther the faster, along a tunable acceleration curve, as Chrome, Firefox and Edge do it on Windows. It picks the scroller under the pointer (chained to the document, both axes) and never starts over a link (which opens in a new tab), a text field or a scroll bar, nor while the page cancels the middle-button mousedown. Hold mode (keep the button down) and toggle mode (a quick tap latches it until the next click) are both live; a direction cursor and an anchor marker show while it runs; a click, Escape, a wheel notch or the pointer leaving ends it. The about: pages, the chrome's lists and the developer tools inherit it from the toolkit (`ui.set_autoscroll_enabled`); the history tree keeps its own middle-button panning. Preferences for the top speed, dead zone, distance, curve and mode; a "View" command toggles it.
