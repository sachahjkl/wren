Selecting text while the page works

This page keeps its own thread busy on purpose. Press anywhere in the text below and drag: the highlight should stay under your pointer, while the pink dot freezes and the numbers above stop counting. The green dot belongs to the compositor and never stops.

The press itself is the page's to answer, so it can wait for the end of a task. Once a drag has begun, each move is worked out from the last layout, and the page hears about the result when its turn comes.

Move the slider to make the tasks longer. Tick the box above to give the page a mousemove listener: moves must then reach the script first, and the highlight waits for the main thread, as it must.

Plain paragraphs

Short lines, then long ones

Drag across all of these slowly, then fast. Go up and down over the line breaks, and back past where you started.

A long task does not announce itself. It arrives between two frames, takes the thread, and until it ends nothing written in script can run: no timers, no listeners, no animation frames. For the person at the screen this is the moment a site feels stuck, even if it is doing exactly what it was asked to do.

Most of that feeling comes from small things. A caret that stops blinking. A scroll bar that does not follow the hand. A selection that jumps forward in one piece after half a second. None of them needs the script's permission, and a browser that waits for it anyway makes the page look worse than it is.

So the work is divided. What depends on the page's code waits for the page. What only depends on what was last laid out can go on, and the two meet again at the next turn of the loop, in the order things happened, as if the script had been there all along.

Words of every length

Double click a word, triple click a paragraph, then drag from there: the selection should grow by whole words or whole paragraphs. Try it on antidisestablishmentarianism, on a-hyphenated-compound-word, on emphasised words, on strong ones, on inline_code() and on a link in the middle of a sentence that lights up under the pointer.

The serif paragraph is here for its different widths. Reading is a rhythm, and a selection that lags breaks it: the eye goes to the end of the line and finds nothing there yet. A long sentence such as this one, which keeps adding clause after clause while the thread stays occupied, gives the highlight plenty of room to fall behind, and shows at once whether it does.

Two columns

A drag that leaves one column for the other has to follow the reading order, not the straight line under the pointer.

Text in columns is laid out in fragments, one box per column. The end of the first column and the top of the second sit far apart on the screen and next to each other in the document, and a selection has to know which of the two it is talking about.

Keep dragging down this column and across to the next one. The highlight should run down to the bottom, start again at the top on the right, and never fill the gap in between, even while the main thread is away.

Then go back the other way, which is where a mistake would show: the focus passes behind the anchor, and what was selected on one side of it lets go.
